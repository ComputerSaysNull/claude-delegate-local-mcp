# Documentation audit — 2026-08-27

First recorded audit, so `docs/audits/` starts here. Run on request rather than because the
gate asked: at 26 commits the audit-due counter was nowhere near its threshold of 60, and no
per-document pressure warning had fired. Immediately after M3 merged (`b2879c2`), which is
the point where staleness is most likely and cheapest to fix.

Scope: every hand-written document, both planes. Checked for staleness against the code,
facts living in more than one place, generated documents out of sync with their sources,
`Docs-Gate-Skip` abuse, size-budget pressure, and cross-reference integrity.

## Found and fixed

Both in `docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md`, and both in the same section — the one M3 changed most.

**A symptom entry for something that cannot happen.** `### HTTP 400 ... thinking_token_budget`
claimed the error was "feature-detected and dropped automatically". Nothing ever detected it:
the adapter has never sent the field, so the endpoint has never had cause to refuse it
through this server. The entry was wrong on the day it was written, and describing absent
machinery in the present tense is exactly what a symptom index must not do — a reader
debugging a real 400 would have gone looking for a handler that does not exist. Deleted
rather than reworded; PLAN.md and ADR-0017 carry why the field is untouched, and this
document owns no facts. M3's probe is what exposed it, which is the argument for probing
before building on a written claim.

**A field name that was never the field.** `### reasoning_exhausted_budget` indexed a symptom
by ADR-0014's internal name for the decision. Callers see `reasoning_exhausted`. A symptom
index keyed on a string that appears in no output is unsearchable by the person who needs it.

While there, the empty-answer section was split in two, because M3 made it two faults with
opposite fixes: exhausted after both mitigations (split the task) versus empty at the lowest
effort with nothing left to step down (shorten it, or raise the cap). Conflating them yields
a confidently wrong instruction — lower an effort that is already lowest. The mechanism now
links to `ARCHITECTURE.md` rather than being restated.

One unrelated defect fixed in passing: a `###` heading with no blank line before it, which
renders as body text rather than a heading, in the `backend_status` connectivity section.

## Found and deliberately not fixed

**PLAN.md cites commit hashes that do not exist on `main`.** Squash-merge discards branch
hashes, so M3's `0b4b633`/`dea7f0b` and M1's `9e03113`/`be4abc7`/`d4a4fad` all dangle. Not
fixed here because the fix is a convention change, not an edit: either record the squash sha
after merge, or cite PR numbers as `CHANGELOG.md` already does and `scripts/release.py`
already backfills. Doing it to M3 alone would leave two conventions in one file, which is
worse than one wrong one. Worth a deliberate pass over the whole file.

## Budget pressure, and a judgement about it

`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` is the one to watch: 422 of 425 lines after two raises inside M3
(340 → 375 → 425). Assessed for splitting and left whole. Every line added was a reason that
does not survive compression — why retry is selective, why the mitigation order is the
inverse of the obvious one, which of the two bounds do not exist yet — and the document owns
`loop.py`, whose behaviour genuinely grew. But it has no room left, and the turn loop lands
in the same file's territory. **Recommendation: split before M4 adds to it**, along the seam
that already exists — a narrative document about the shape of a delegation, and a reference
document about the response state machine. Splitting under pressure mid-milestone is worse
than splitting deliberately first.

`CONTRIBUTING.md` sits at exactly 190/190 and `docs/TROUBLESHOOTING.md` at 268/270 after this
audit's edits (raised from 265, reason recorded in the file). Neither is urgent; both mean the
next addition has to earn its place or trim something, which is the mechanism working.
